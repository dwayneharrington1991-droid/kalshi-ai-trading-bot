## What was wrong and what changed

### 1. Openings withheld for an hour (confirmed root cause, fixed) — `e8e8808`
Evidence (live journal on Oracle, read-only): the openings at 21:30, 21:45, 22:00 and 22:15 UTC on
2026-11-19 were recorded `INSUFFICIENT DATA`, reason `Incomplete 24h window: 1439/1440 closed bars;
missing UTC intervals: 2026-11-19T21:19:00`. The 21:19 candle was absent from both Coinbase candle
APIs (still absent when re-queried on 2026-11-20). The exact-minute trade recovery that exists for this
case could not work:
- the trade endpoint returns HTTP 500 for every RFC3339 `start`/`end` (the code sent RFC3339);
  Unix-second strings work (verified live);
- that minute had 2,477 trades, more than one 1,000-trade page, and a full page was rejected as truncated.

The gap was filled by chance at 22:16; otherwise every opening would have been withheld until the gap
left the 24h window. Fix: Unix-second range, newest-first pagination, de-duplication of the overlapping
second by trade id; conflicting receipts, a non-advancing page or an exhausted 10-page budget fail closed.
No interpolation; the 1440-bar requirement is unchanged. Verified live: the recovered 21:19 bar has
2,477 trades and matches the stored bar's high, low, close and volume (11.16109312) exactly.

### 2. Opening generated 2–3 s after the open (measured, improved) — `2bc07b1`
A read-only probe of Kalshi's public listings found the new contract active at about +0.9 s and its
threshold published between +0.9 s and +1.8 s. Live openings were generated at +2.3 s to +3.3 s
(threshold received at about +1.2 s; the rest is the prediction cycle, p50 collection-to-publish 0.94 s).
The collector now wakes just after each quarter-hour boundary and polls every 0.25 s for 6 s. Expected
gain is a few hundred milliseconds; the remaining delay is the prediction cycle. Every input remains
cut off at the open, so the delay never lets post-open data in.

### 3. UNCLEAR vs WAIT (display fixed; records unchanged) — `a081878`
The headline terminal already showed WAIT. The market-history table, predictions table and settlement
outlook printed the stored label (e.g. "UNCLEAR 55.1% / DOWN 44.9%"). A display-only helper now shows
every non-UP/DOWN call as WAIT. Immutable records and scoring are unchanged (the earlier approach that
rewrote records, 0431cbf, broke two pipeline tests and was reverted).

### 4. Test-suite "hang" and CI — `b3c8c3d`, `3a430d5`
The full suite takes about 16 minutes on Linux (1828 passed, 9 skipped on `eb8747d`), longer than the old
15-minute CI job limit. CI now has a 40-minute limit, a 300 s per-test timeout (a hung test fails by name)
and a resource trace. See "Test results" for the outcome.

## Model selection, scoring and learning
- The learning cursor, eligibility and promotion gate were checked; no defect found. Zero-new-data
  cycles at 22:07 were caused by the withheld openings in item 1, not by the learner.
- The scoreboard excludes the 9 fallback UP/DOWN rows; that matches the dashboard, which showed those
  uncalibrated rows as WAIT.
- **Live out-of-sample evidence for the deployed candidate `b50b9dc30f6d664c`** (281 openings after its
  training cutoff, chronological): Brier 0.2418 vs 0.2505 for a past-only base rate (difference −0.0087,
  95% bootstrap −0.0165 to −0.0006); log loss 0.6766 vs 0.6942. **Second half (since 2026-11-18 08:15):
  0.2462 vs 0.2467 — no measurable edge.** Directional accuracy 64.4% (54.1–73.6%) on 90 calls.
- A fresh chronological retrain on the latest data (179/59/61 split) loses to the train prior on the
  untouched test block (Brier 0.2450 vs 0.2422). The existing gate correctly keeps it out. **No model
  was promoted or deployed.**
- No claim of accuracy or profitability is made. The evidence supports at most a small, fading edge.

### 5. CI runner killed by the retirement rehearsal (root cause, fixed) — `3ce9071`
Three CI runs died near 50% with "The runner has received a shutdown signal". A resource trace ruled
out memory (1.3 of 16 GB). Cause: on a GitHub runner every process is inside the runner's `.service`
unit, so `retire_old_bots.sh` (exercised by `tests/test_retire_old_bots.py` on stand-in ports) read that
unit from its stand-in's cgroup and ran `systemctl disable --now` on it, stopping the runner. From cron it
would have disabled `crond.service`. The script and `production_restart` now never treat the caller's own
unit as the target's manager. The live 8768 process runs in an SSH session scope and is unaffected.

### Rollback
- **Automatic:** any failed gate, soak or postflight restores the exact previous process
  (`DEPLOY ROLLED BACK` is printed). Nothing to do.
- **If the window closes mid-run:** rerun the same command with `-ResumeJobId <id printed at start>`;
  it never starts a second job.
- **Manual rollback after a successful deploy** (from PowerShell; uses the run id printed in the job log,
  or the newest folder in `~/.kalshi-deploy/preview-8768/runs`):

